# Code of conduct

Vespertine's issues, discussions and pull requests are for people who care about how their music sounds. Keep them a place where anyone can ask a question, report a problem or send a fix without being made to feel small.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good faith. People are here because they want the same thing you do.
- Disagree about the code, the audio and the evidence, never about the person.
- Welcome newcomers. Nobody should need to know what DoP or hog mode is before asking.
- Back claims about sound with something others can check: a measurement, a file, the signal path, a null test. "It sounds better" is welcome as an impression, not as proof.
- Accept a decision once it's made, even if it isn't the one you wanted.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or personal or political attacks.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained trolling or derailing, including relitigating settled audiophile debates in unrelated threads.
- Spam, and promoting paid products in threads that aren't about them.

## Enforcement

The maintainer, Alexander Szeremeta ([@szeremeta1](https://github.com/szeremeta1)), moderates every Vespertine space. To report a problem privately, contact him through GitHub. Reports are kept confidential.

Depending on how serious it is, the response can be a reminder, a hidden or edited comment, a temporary block, or a permanent ban from the project. Anyone asked to stop is expected to stop straight away.

This code applies in all of Vespertine's spaces on GitHub, and when someone represents the project elsewhere. It is adapted from the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org), version 2.1.

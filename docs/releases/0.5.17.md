**Dolby Atmos and surround formats, DTS CDs in 5.1, 32-bit integer output, and steady playback from busy servers.**

### New
- **Dolby Atmos.** Dolby Digital Plus with Atmos is rendered by macOS's own Atmos renderer on the output you chose, the way Apple Music plays it: head-tracked Spatial Audio on AirPods, height channels on a multichannel output. Settings → Surround formats can instead play its 5.1/7.1 bed through Nocturne's own signal path.
- **Dolby Digital and Dolby Digital Plus** (`.ac3`, `.ec3`, and Dolby audio in M4A/MP4) play with every channel in its place.
- **DTS-HD Master Audio, Dolby TrueHD and DTS** in `.dts`, `.dtshd`, `.thd` and Matroska (`.mka`) files. TrueHD and DTS-HD MA are lossless and can play bit-perfect. For DTS:X and TrueHD Atmos, Nocturne plays the channel bed (the objects need a receiver).
- **Send Dolby and DTS to an AV receiver.** A new per-output switch ("Send Dolby and DTS to a receiver") sends Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus (Atmos included, over HDMI) and DTS CDs untouched, in the standard IEC 61937 format, for a receiver or soundbar to decode. Only turn it on for an output that has one.
- **Integer mode.** With exclusive access, on DACs that support it (the FiiO K11 does), music that needs no processing goes to the DAC as 32-bit integers with no floating-point step. 32-bit recordings now play bit-perfect too. Settings → Output.
- **DTS CDs and DTS-WAV files are decoded to 5.1.** These files store a DTS surround bitstream disguised as 16-bit stereo audio. Nocturne used to play that data as-is, which comes out as extremely loud noise. Now it recognizes the DTS stream and decodes it with FFmpeg's DTS decoder (built into Nocturne, verified sample for sample against FFmpeg on a real disc). The files are listed as DTS 5.1, play in surround on multichannel outputs or as Spatial Audio on AirPods, and CUE-split tracks start exactly where they should.

### Fixed
- **Songs playing from a network share no longer stop for a few seconds mid-track.** The cause was the server, not the network. When its disks are busy (downloads, seeding, media-server scans), a single read can wait several seconds, longer than the 5 seconds of audio Nocturne kept in hand. Now:
  - Nocturne keeps about 20–30 seconds of audio ahead instead of 5.
  - It copies the playing track to your Mac straight away, and as soon as the copy is complete, playback moves to it mid-song. It picks up at the exact sample, so there's nothing to hear, and the rest of the song no longer touches the network. This covers lossless files (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AIFF and similar) and DSD sent as DoP.
  - If a read still stalls long enough to empty the buffer, Nocturne waits until 5 seconds are buffered again before resuming (2 before), so one slow patch doesn't turn into a string of stops.
- A slow but working server is no longer mistaken for a dead connection. Before, two slow answers in a row made Nocturne remount the share, which cut off the song that was playing.
- **Albums are dated by their original release.** Reissues and remasters carry their own date in the release-date tag, so *Back in Black* showed as 2003 and *Born to Run* as 2014. When a file has an original-release-date tag, the album's year and decade now come from it. The release date itself is still kept.
- **Covers embedded as a `METADATA_BLOCK_PICTURE` comment now show.** Some taggers store FLAC cover art this way.
- **A cover image in a multi-disc album's main folder is found** for the tracks in its `CD 01`, `Disc 2`, … folders.
- More spellings of the same genre are grouped together: Hardrock / Hard Rock, Pop/Rock / Pop Rock, Classique / Classica / Classical.
- Re-reading a file that hasn't changed no longer loses its hi-res analysis.
- Raw Dolby and DTS streams, which have no tag format of their own, are added to the library from their file names. Matroska files use their tags.

**Network shares, fake hi-res detection, and finding your music.**

### New
- **Connect to Server… (⌘K).** Play music from a NAS, a file server or another Mac, at home or from anywhere over Tailscale or a VPN.
  - Works with SMB, NFS and WebDAV, as a guest or a registered user. Passwords live in your login keychain, shared with Finder.
  - Shares are **read-only by default**, so Nocturne never changes files on the server.
  - Nocturne reconnects by itself at launch, after sleep and when your network or VPN comes back. Offline shares keep their albums; nothing is marked missing.
  - **Fast indexing over slow links.** Tags are read with a few large reads per file instead of dozens of small ones, spread across folders. A 13,000-track, 550 GB library indexes in about ten minutes over a 100 ms connection, and later rescans take seconds.
  - **Local cache** (Settings → Network): the playing track and the next few are copied in the background, within a size you choose. **Keep Offline** keeps whole albums for when the server can't be reached.
- **Fake hi-res detection** (Analysis tab). Nocturne now recognizes files made from MP3, AAC or Opus sources, and a new verdict, **Synthetic high frequencies**, catches "enhanced" and "remastered" files whose highs were generated by SBR or AI upscalers rather than recorded.
  - Every verdict shows its evidence (cutoff, drop, how consistently it appears, the synthetic band), with a confidence level and markers on the spectrum.
  - The thresholds were calibrated on genuine CD and hi-res masters against the same music through MP3, AAC, Opus, HE-AAC, upsampling and padding. See [how it works](https://github.com/szeremeta1/Nocturne/blob/main/docs/ANALYSIS.md).
- **Analysis results are saved** and appear instantly. The Analysis tab follows the track you selected or the one playing, whichever changed last.
- **Analyze new music automatically** (Settings → Library), an **Analyze Library** command (⌥⌘A) and **Analyze** on any album. The Songs view can filter by verdict or show tracks not yet analyzed.
- **Mac sound output follows Nocturne while playing** (Settings → Playback, on by default). Volume keys and headphone controls, such as the AirPods Max Digital Crown, then adjust the device you're listening to rather than another one selected in Control Center.
- **Find Music on This Mac…** (⇧⌘F) searches every indexed drive with Spotlight and checks each file's real format. Voice recordings and short clips are left out, and duplicates are recognized.
- **Import & Organize with APFS clones.** Copies go into `~/Music/Nocturne` as Artist / Album / Track and take no extra space on the same drive. Untagged files are named from structured file names.
- **Enrich Metadata…** (⇧⌘E) fills in missing titles, artists, years, genres and cover art from file names and MusicBrainz. Only near-certain matches are pre-selected.

### Improved
- A broad reliability audit hardened the real-time audio path, playback lifetime, queue edits with shuffle and repeat, seeking, CUE sheets, imports and tag editing (undo now restores the exact file and refuses to overwrite files changed elsewhere). A damaged library opens a recovery screen instead of quitting.
- A larger output buffer rides out network hiccups.
- The signal path never claims bit-perfect when channels are dropped or DoP isn't really in use.

### Fixed
- Natural roll-offs in lo-fi and older recordings were reported as "lossy origin". Earlier results are cleared and re-analyzed with the new method.
- A crash when scanning folders with many MP3 files.
- Release dates on WAV, AIFF and MP3 files could be dropped or shifted by a year.
- Temporary MusicBrainz outages are retried instead of being reported as "no match".

### Install
Download **Nocturne-0.4.0.dmg**, or let Nocturne 0.2 update itself. Requires macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon or Intel. Signed with a Developer ID, notarized and stapled.

**Vespertine 0.14.1 adds a Visualizer window: the music drawn live in eight styles, starting with a spinning record, in a window of its own that never touches the sound going to your DAC.**

### The Visualizer window
- **A separate window.** Choose Window > Visualizer (Option-Command-V) or click the new toolbar button. It is its own window, so it can sit on another display, go full screen there and keep running while the main window is minimized. It remembers its display, its size and whether it was full screen. If its display is gone it opens on the one you have.
- **Off by default.** Turn it on in Settings > Playback > Visualizer. Until then there is no menu item, no toolbar button and no work done. Turning it off closes the window. With the window closed, Vespertine does nothing extra while it plays.
- **Seven colour themes and a sensitivity control** for every style. **Vespertine** is the new default theme, made from the app's own colors: obsidian, copper, brass and ivory. The others are Brass, Neon, Aurora, Ember, Ice and Monochrome. The controls fade after a few seconds, and keys 1 to 8 pick a style.

### Eight styles
- **Vinyl (the default).** The album cover is the label of a record turning at 33⅓ or 45 RPM, with grooves, the gaps between songs, dust and a platter with strobe dots under a lamp. A ring of light around the platter shows the spectrum. The tonearm follows the song from the lead-in to the run-out, and the record spins up when you press play and slows to a stop on pause.
- **Spectrum.** Bars from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, 32 to 128 of them, with peak caps that fall away. Heights in dB or in linear amplitude.
- **Spectrogram.** Frequency up, time across, with level shown as color. It keeps 6 to 30 seconds of history.
- **Club.** A core that swells with the bass, a circular spectrum, rings sent out on every beat, turning lasers and a tunnel of rings. See the strobe below.
- **Scope.** A phosphor-screen look. As a goniometer (the default) it plots left against right, so mono is an upright line and out-of-phase sound lies across, with a correlation bar under it. As an oscilloscope it shows left and right against time. Its gain follows the music, so quiet and loud recordings fill the screen alike.
- **Meters.** Two analogue meters with needles that move like the real ones: **VU** (IEC 60268-17) and **PPM** (the BBC meter, IEC 60268-10 Type IIa). The reference level is Automatic by default, or fixed at -20, -18, -14 or -10 dBFS.
- **Particles.** 20,000 particles drifting on a flow that never clumps. The bass makes the whole field breathe, each beat sends out a ripple, and the highs make it sparkle.
- **Mirrored.** The bar spectrum mirrored around a circle with the album cover in the middle, or up and down from a line.

### Club strobe, with a warning
- **Off by default.** The Club style can flash the whole window on strong beats. Turning it on for the first time shows a Photosensitivity Warning that you have to accept. Flashing lights can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy. If you or anyone watching has epilepsy or has had a seizure, leave it off.
- **Limits.** It flashes at most 2.5 times a second, under the 3 a second that photosensitivity guidelines allow. It never flashes while Reduce Motion or Dim Flashing Lights is on in System Settings > Accessibility > Display. The switch is greyed out and says why. The same switch is in Settings > Playback > Visualizer.

### Bit-perfect stays bit-perfect
- **A copy, not a detour.** The visualizer reads a copy of the samples after room correction and the equalizer. The samples going to your DAC are never changed or delayed, and a test checks that the output is bit-for-bit identical with the visualizer on and off.
- **Volume doesn't shrink the picture.** The copy is scaled back for digital volume and ReplayGain, so the picture is the same size at any volume.
- **When there is nothing to draw.** Dolby and DTS bitstreams carry no audio Vespertine can look at, so the window shows nothing. The same goes for Atmos rendered by macOS. With more than two channels it draws the front left and right.
- **Light on your Mac.** The analysis runs on its own thread and the drawing runs on the GPU at your display's refresh rate, up to 120 Hz. It stops while the window is minimized or fully covered.

### Also new
- **44 languages.** Every new string is translated into all 44 languages Vespertine ships in.
- **Documentation.** How it works, the strobe rules and the meters are written up in `docs/VISUALIZER.md`.

### Verification
- The engine, analysis and app test suites and the full audit script pass. New tests cover the copy of the samples (including that the output is identical with it on and off, and a reader racing the audio thread for 4 million frames), the analysis, the VU and PPM ballistics, the strobe rules, the window's remembered position and every style drawing. The real-time audio code is also checked under the address, thread and undefined-behavior sanitizers.
- Not yet checked: that pressing Return in the strobe warning chooses Cancel; that macOS tells Vespertine the moment Dim Flashing Lights changes (the window re-reads it whenever Vespertine becomes active); the new translations with native speakers (they are machine-quality); and the new styles measured in a Release build of the app.

### Install
Download **Vespertine-0.14.1.dmg**, or let Vespertine update itself. Requires macOS 14.4 or later on Apple silicon or Intel. Signed with a Developer ID, notarized and stapled. Homebrew: `brew upgrade --cask vespertine`.

**Vespertine 1.2.0.** The free, open-source Mac player for surround and hi-res music collections.

If you're new here: Vespertine plays your stereo and surround music from one library (FLAC, DSD, SACD and DVD-Video images, DTS CDs, Dolby TrueHD, DTS-HD Master Audio, Atmos from Dolby Digital Plus), shows every step between the file and your DAC, measures and corrects your room with a USB microphone, and tells you whether your "hi-res" files really are. No account, no telemetry, no subscription.

### New

- **Network Players (UPnP, beta).** Off by default, in Settings › Playback. Turned on, Vespertine finds UPnP AV and OpenHome players on your network (moOde, Volumio, streamers) and lists them as outputs. A player gets the original file; if the equalizer, room correction, ReplayGain or Vespertine's volume is in use, it gets 24-bit FLAC made after that processing, and the signal path says so. Turned off, Vespertine looks for nothing and opens no port.
- **AirPlay Players (beta).** Off by default, in Settings › Playback. Turned on, Vespertine finds AirPlay 1 receivers and sends them 16-bit / 44.1 kHz ALAC: CD-quality files as their own samples, everything else converted, and the signal path says which. macOS's own AirPlay outputs are untouched.
- **Receive from Other Apps (beta).** Off by default, in Settings › Playback. Turned on, other apps on your network can send music to Vespertine, which plays it on the output you chose with the same signal path as a file. Control apps such as mconnect and BubbleUPnP list this Mac as a player; the ones that sign in to TIDAL or Qobuz themselves can play those services through it. Vespertine itself has no code for any streaming service. Your own queue is kept and comes back when the app stops. Only http and https addresses are fetched, and nothing is kept afterwards. Tested against Vespertine's own test control point, not yet against those apps: reports wanted. [Details](https://github.com/szeremeta1/Vespertine/blob/main/docs/STREAMING.md).
- **Music Servers (OpenSubsonic, beta).** Off by default, in Settings › Library. Turned on, the music on your own server (Navidrome, Gonic, Airsonic and others that speak the open OpenSubsonic API) gets its own section in the sidebar, and plays through the same engine and signal path. Vespertine asks for the original file; if a server converts it anyway, the signal path says "Converted by the server" and the badge is not BIT-PERFECT. A format the server only lists is marked "≈" until the song has played. Your sign-in is kept in the macOS Keychain and nowhere else. No offline copies yet. Tested against Vespertine's own test server, not yet against real ones: reports wanted. [Details](https://github.com/szeremeta1/Vespertine/blob/main/docs/STREAMING.md).
- **Local Library switch.** Settings › Library has a new switch, on by default, for listeners who play only from a music server or from other apps. Off, the music in your folders, drives and network shares is in no list, search, playlist, queue or count, a song from them that is playing stops, and nothing scans, watches or connects to a share. Nothing is deleted: turn it back on and your folders, play counts, favorites and playlists are as you left them. Find Music, Add Folder and the other adding commands are greyed out in the File menu while it is off. A share that was already mounted stays mounted until Vespertine quits.
- **Balance.** Each output has its own left/right balance. The BAL chip beside the volume opens a slider with a stop at the center and a Center Balance button; in the Controls menu, Balance Left, Balance Right and Center Balance (⌃⌘←, ⌃⌘→, ⌃⌘0) move it 5 points at a time. Only the far side is turned down, so nothing is ever made louder and a centered balance changes nothing.
- **Setup.** A new step, **More ways to play**, offers the four switches above, each off until you choose it. Files stay where they are unless you choose "Copy and organize", the Extras page says which switches start on, the Sound step says where balance is, and the last step offers Add Music when the library is still empty. Choices, switches and steps are now named for VoiceOver, and the list of outputs shows when there is more below.

### Fixed

- A music server that stops answering mid-song is named in the notice ("Home Server" isn't answering. Still trying…), not the name of a scratch folder. Vespertine gives up 15 seconds after the server's last byte every time, Now Playing says why playback stopped until you play something, and the phone remote keeps that reason for as long as the Mac shows it.
- A WAV or AIFF song on a network share that stops answering mid-song now says so, instead of ending early as if the song were over.
- The first Play after a network player had sat unused now plays, or says why not. A new album started after a pause plays at the first press (a player that answers "Action Failed" once is asked again).
- Dolby Atmos through macOS's renderer could crash when a seek or a stop arrived while the file was being read. It no longer does.
- 38 messages and labels in room measurement and song info showed in English in every language; they are translated now.

### iPhone and iPad

The iPhone and iPad app is a separate download and ships on its own schedule. The Mac side of what it does now: the phone can play the Mac's library itself (Output › This iPhone), finds the Mac from away from home, and gets the original file where the phone can play it and lossless PCM where it can't.

### Install

Download **Vespertine-1.2.0.dmg**, or `brew install --cask szeremeta1/tap/vespertine`. macOS 14.4 or later, Apple silicon and Intel. Signed with a Developer ID, notarized and stapled.

Already installed? Vespertine updates itself, or run `brew upgrade --cask vespertine`.

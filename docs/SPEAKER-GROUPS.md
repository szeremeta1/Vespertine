# Speaker Groups (beta)

Two or more USB DACs played as one multichannel system: for example a Fosi ZD3 and a FiiO K11 as the front and back pairs of a quad. This is a beta. It is off by default, and with it off nothing about playback changes.

**Status.** The group engine, the routing, the editor and the room-correction pieces are covered by automated tests, and by opt-in tests against virtual devices (Vespertine Loopback and BlackHole). It has also been tested by hand on a real pair of DACs (a Fosi Audio ZD3 leader and a FiiO K11 follower) in several configurations and settings. One earlier session on that pair (with a UMIK-1), using a probe tool rather than the app, showed a group can be built, held and played, and measured how the DACs start (see [What is not verified](#what-is-not-verified)).

## Turning it on

Settings › Playback › **Speaker Groups (beta)** holds one switch, off by default (`AppSettings.speakerGroupsFeature`, like Transitions'), with a line saying what it does. Turning it on shows the list of groups under it and takes effect at once, with no restart; turning it off takes the list, the groups in the output list and the groups handed to the engine away again. Off, the app reads no groups, offers none and hands the engine none. Turning it off while a group is the chosen output lets go of that output (the system's default plays); the saved groups stay in their file for the next time. The old `defaults write org.szeremeta.Vespertine speakerGroupsFeature -bool YES` still sets the same switch.

## How it works, and what that means for bit-perfect

- **A private aggregate.** Vespertine asks Core Audio for an aggregate device made of your DACs. It exists only inside Vespertine: other apps cannot see it or play into it, and it is removed when playback stops.
- **One DAC leads.** Each USB DAC runs on its own crystal; two DACs differ by tens of parts per million (the Fosi measures +17.4 ppm against the UMIK-1), which would move the stereo image within seconds. So one DAC, the clock leader, sets the time, and every other DAC is resampled continuously by a few millionths to follow it (Apple's drift correction, at its highest quality).
- **Only the leader can be bit-perfect.** The leader's channels get the file's samples untouched. A drift-corrected DAC's samples are always changed, however slightly: on virtual devices it measured 103 to 131 dB signal to noise at 17.4 and 100 ppm, far below the noise of a real DAC and amplifier, but not bit-perfect. A group is labelled BIT-PERFECT only when every channel the song uses lands on a DAC that is not resampled (the leader, or DACs that share a hardware clock) and nothing else changes the samples: no mixing, level, filter or digital volume. Otherwise the signal path says what changed. A whole-frame delay only shifts samples, so the leader can stay bit-perfect with one.
- **Zero offset, not zero latency.** The goal is that every speaker reaches the seat at the same moment, to a fraction of a millisecond, not that sound is instant.
- **PCM only.** DSD is converted to PCM (the path says so), Dolby and DTS bitstreams are decoded, and integer mode and "Match 16-bit sources" are off for groups. At most 16 output channels in total.
- **Every DAC is held.** With exclusive access on, each member is held and set to the song's rate; the aggregate itself cannot be held.
- **Every DAC at its best format.** Each member is switched to the deepest integer format it offers (32-bit where it has it), as a single DAC is, and on quit it gets what the "when Vespertine quits" setting says, as a single DAC does. Some DACs come up in 16-bit after they are plugged in; left there, the leader could not be bit-perfect for a 24-bit song and a resampled DAC would be cut to 16 bits.

## Setting up a group

Settings › Playback › Speaker Groups (beta), once it is on, lists, duplicates and removes groups; the editor has:

1. **Devices and clock leader.** The leader is the DAC whose channels matter most (usually the front pair): it is never resampled.
2. **Channel map.** Every channel of every DAC against the speaker on it (front left, centre, back right…), with an **Identify** button per channel that plays a quiet tone (three bursts of pink noise at -30 dBFS) on that channel of that DAC only. Identify plays on the DAC itself, and is refused while the group is playing, because its DACs are held then. Each channel also has an **Inverted** box.
3. **The room.** A top-down drawing: drag the speakers and the seat. The distance, angle, delay and level of each speaker are worked out from it.
4. **Policies.** What stereo music does on a quad (front pair only is the default; mirrored to the back at a level; each pair its own stereo; every speaker), what the LFE channel becomes where there is no subwoofer (dropped by default), and what happens when a DAC goes away (pause, or carry on with the rest).
5. **Volume.** See below.

## What plays

- **Routing by position.** A song's channels are put onto the group's speakers by where the speakers stand: a channel with a speaker of its own goes there untouched; one between two speakers is panned between them with equal power (halfway is -3 dB each, as in ITU-R BS.775); one outside every pair goes to the nearest at -3 dB. A 7.1 song on a group with one surround pair keeps both of its surround pairs there: the pair with no speakers of its own (the sides, on a group with a back pair) joins the other on the surround speaker of its side at -3 dB, and none of it goes to the fronts. Mono is a centre. The ring is as wide as the group, so stereo then 5.1 does not reopen the output. The 4.0 rows are derived from those rules, not from a standard; they need listening approval.
- **Delays.** Each speaker waits for the farthest, and the measured start offset between the DACs, when known, is a delay too (macOS ignores an aggregate's extra latency, so the offset is held as whole frames in the room-correction chain). A speaker's own delay trim is added. Only the electronic part of a DAC's lateness is held: a drift-corrected DAC is held back by the resampler's measured delay (53.5 frames, 1.115 ms at 48 kHz and quality 127, from the hardware session of 2026-11-07), and the wizard's measured skew replaces that with the DAC's whole measured delay less the speakers' path difference from the drawing (the drawing's distances are already equalised above, so they must not be counted twice). A measured skew is kept with the drift setting, quality and rate it was measured under and is not used if the DAC now runs differently. A room profile measured on the group already holds the delay between the DACs (its sweeps are timed against the leader), so while the group runs as it did when measured, the skew is not added a second time; trims, polarity and the rate trim still are. Nothing else is guessed.
- **Level trims and polarity.** Each speaker's trim (-40 to +12 dB) and **Inverted** flag are played in the same chain, once, whichever room profile is chosen. A flip turns the speaker's signal over (every sample's sign), after the delay and before any filters.
- **Headroom guard.** Positive trims can push a loud song past full scale, so the chain's shared preamp is lowered by the largest boost any channel can reach (its trim plus the top of its filters, rounded up to 0.1 dB). Cuts need none, and a room profile that already leaves room keeps its own preamp. The balance between speakers holds: with +6 dB on one speaker, that speaker plays at the file's level and the others 6 dB below it. Not covered: a measured filter (impulse response).
- **Headroom for a fold.** A song with more channels than the group has speakers has channels summed onto one speaker: 5.1 on a quad puts the centre on the fronts (1.71 times full scale at worst, +4.6 dB), 7.1 also puts the side surrounds on the back pair. Such a speaker has a limiter (the one Fill the room uses): its samples are the routed ones, exactly, until it would clip, and only then is it turned down, over 1.5 ms of look-ahead, and let back up over 200 ms. The song's level is not lowered to make room. A speaker that plays one channel of the file at 1.0 is never limited, and ReplayGain is allowed for. Not covered: sound from other apps through Mac Sound Output, which is routed in the I/O cycle with no delay to spare.
- Any trim, flip or lowered preamp on an output that carries music makes the path ROOM CORRECTION, not BIT-PERFECT; on an output that stays silent it changes nothing.

## Fill the room

A switch in the group's editor, off by default, for a room where people listen from anywhere and not only from the seat. With it on, every speaker plays the whole song or the whole film:

- Each speaker keeps what the routing gives it (the front pair plays a stereo song's own left and right, a surround channel stays on its speaker) and adds the rest of its side of the room as a fill: a back speaker gets the front channel and the centre, a front speaker gets the surround channel. So dialogue is heard next to a back speaker, and a stereo song next to any speaker. A speaker never plays the same channel twice.
- The fill is 3 dB under the routed sound, 15 to 18 ms later, and passed through a short all-pass chain that differs for each speaker. At the seat the sound therefore still comes from the front (the first arrival decides where a sound seems to come from), and two speakers playing the same channel do not cancel each other as much at spots between them.
- A stereo song's ambience (what its two channels do not share) is added to the surround pair. Bass below 90 Hz from the routed sound plays in mono on the subwoofer, or on the front pair when there is none; the fill carries no bass.
- A limiter on each speaker keeps a fill added to a loud channel from clipping. It does nothing to a speaker that stays within full scale.
- What other apps play through [Mac Sound Output (beta)](MAC-SOUND-OUTPUT.md) gets the same fill as a plain mix, with no delay, all-pass or limiter, so that a film stays in step with its picture.

Under the switch there are two styles. **Stage in front** is everything above, and is what a group gets when the switch is turned on. **Even everywhere** is for a room where no seat matters more than the rest: the fill plays at the routed sound's own level and at the same time, with no all-pass and no ambience, so every speaker of a side plays the same thing. That is the most even around the room; at the seat the sound is all around the listener and not in front, and between two speakers playing the same channel some frequencies cancel more than with the first style. Bass management and the limiter are the same in both.

While the switch is on, it takes the place of the group's choice for stereo songs in Vespertine's own playback, and nothing the group plays is bit-perfect.

What it cannot do: a few speakers cannot make every spot in a room the best seat. The image a stereo pair draws exists only between its two speakers; anywhere else a listener hears the nearest speaker first. Fill the room makes sure that what they hear there is the whole song at a similar level. It does not measure the room: the speakers' levels are the ones set in the group. It has not been measured or listened to on real DACs yet.

## Room correction on a group

Open Room Correction with the group as the output:

- **The drawing as a profile.** "Use the Drawing" makes a profile of delays and levels from the drawing (no filters; no microphone needed), binds the group to it and switches the output to play with it. Once it exists it follows the drawing: moving a speaker updates it. A measured or imported profile is never touched by that.
- **Measured against drawn.** When a measured profile and the drawing disagree about a speaker by more than 0.25 ms (about 9 cm of air), the window says which speaker and that the drawing or the cabling may be off. 0.25 ms is a little above the follower's creep seen in the hardware session, and far above the measurement's own noise; it is not a proven bound.
- **Measuring.** The wizard measures the group as the layout the drawing makes (a quad is a quad) on the group's outputs, saved for the group's output, with the timing tones from a speaker on the clock leader. The group plays silence for 15 seconds first (the follower crept about 0.2 ms in the first 15 s of the hardware session), and a silent hold keeps the aggregate running between sweeps so the followers stay settled. Before the sweeps the wizard times each follower against the leader through the same microphone (five short captures for the delay, one 60-second capture for the rate) and saves the result with the group; a follower it can't time is left as it was and the wizard says so. The follower's chirp is played 11 dB louder than the leader's (it arrives quieter), within 1 dB of full scale, and a capture 100 frames or more from the others is a later copy of the chirp and is left out.
- **Rate trim.** With drift correction on, the FiiO K11 still fell behind the Fosi ZD3 at a steady +13.5 ppm (0.81 ms a minute) for the 2 minutes measured; the settings the code chooses (the leader is the aggregate's main sub-device, quality 127 is Core Audio's maximum, every member is held at the same rate) show no cause, so the wizard measures that rate and the room chain brings the creeping DAC forward by it: its delay line is read slightly faster than it is written (a 16-tap windowed-sinc read, on that DAC's channels only, which are resampled anyway). Every channel is held back a little at the start (8 ms for 13.5 ppm) to give it room, and the trim lasts 10 minutes of a play; after that the DAC drifts again. A leader channel stays a whole-frame delay and can stay bit-perfect, unless a follower measured ahead of the leader makes the leader the one brought forward: its channels are then read between samples and the signal path says ROOM CORRECTION. Not yet checked on the hardware: that the trim holds the pair within the tolerance for 10 minutes, and what happens past the FiiO's clock change at about 7 minutes.

## Volume

A group has no volume of its own in Vespertine. The volume keys do not control it while Vespertine plays it (a group published with [Mac Sound Output (beta)](MAC-SOUND-OUTPUT.md) is an output of the Mac with a volume the keys set, for what other apps play). Each DAC's own volume is set once, by hand: the editor has a slider for each DAC that has a volume Core Audio can set, written when it is let go, never saved or applied by Vespertine itself. The speakers' trims and the optional master volume (the in-app digital volume) are digital and make playback not bit-perfect.

## When a DAC goes away

With "pause", the group pauses and names the DAC that went away; with "carry on", it re-plans on the DACs left and folds the music onto them after a short mute. A lost leader pauses. When the DAC returns, the group is rebuilt at the next play. It never carries on silently on a new clock in the middle of a song.

## What is not verified

- Hand testing on the Fosi Audio ZD3 and FiiO K11 is not a measurement. Not yet measured from the app on real DACs: bit-exact leader, follower quality, quad channel for channel, the Identify tone, the sliders, a real unplug, one hour of playback. [verification/hardware/SPEAKER-GROUPS.md](../verification/hardware/SPEAKER-GROUPS.md) is the script for the next session.
- From the 11-06 session: start offsets are repeatable to about the measurement's noise (σ 21 µs) in typical runs, but 3 of 49 drift-corrected runs came in about 3.5 ms (166 frames) late, unexplained; the follower crept about 0.2 ms between 1.6 s and 15.6 s and had not flattened (the 2026-11-07 mic session: a steady +13.5 ppm for 2 minutes, not a settling); the Fosi against FiiO drift over 300 s was not measured.
- The Room Portrait in the wizard still draws its stereo/5.1 sketch for a quad.
- The words of Speaker Groups (the settings, the editor, the room drawing, the engine's labels, notes and refusals, and the group part of the measurement wizard) are in the 44-language catalog. They are machine translations that native speakers have not reviewed yet. Still English: the signal-path lines for a group (`GroupSignalPath`, `GroupOutputRows`) and the engine's reason texts in `GroupRouting` and `GroupFormatRules`.

# Comparison sources and scope

The [README table](../README.md#compared-with-other-mac-players) and [website table](https://vespertineapp.com/#compare) cover the same features. "Not documented" means the vendor's own documentation (or, for open-source players, the source code) doesn't say either way. It is not a claim that a player lacks the feature. No prices or overall sound-quality rankings are included. Corrections welcome through [GitHub issues](https://github.com/szeremeta1/Vespertine/issues).

The starting point was the maintainer's October 9, 2026 competitor-review summary. The feature rows follow that review: file formats, output control, local surround, Spatial Audio, analysis, source availability, SACD ISO, convolution/plugins and streaming services. The primary pages below were checked for this documentation update; cells were filled again on October 9, 2026 from each product's own documentation, change log or source code. Every remaining `?` was researched again on October 14, 2026 against the same kinds of primary source. A `Yes` or `No` appears only where one of those says so, or where a product's complete user guide or source code shows the feature is absent; "vendor claim" marks a product's own bit-perfect statement that this page has not measured. A documented feature is not a Vespertine hardware test of a competitor.

## Vespertine

The [feature details](FEATURES.md), [signal-path conditions](../Packages/VespertineKit/Sources/VespertineAudio/SignalPath.swift) and [verification guide](VERIFICATION.md) describe what the app implements and how it is checked. The table says “conditions + tests” rather than claiming an independent loopback result.

Receiver bitstream and multichannel DAC output are experimental, untested on real receivers and multichannel USB DACs (one Sony TV over HDMI has been tested: see docs/DEVICES.md); reports wanted. Decoding, routing and carrier tests do not establish hardware compatibility. SACD ISO playback is new (two real discs tested against sacd_extract). There is no convolution, audio effect plugin hosting or streaming-service integration.

## Apple Music on Mac

The supplied October 2026 review reports third-party Nativerate loopback tests on an RME interface: bit-exact 16/24-bit PCM from 44.1 to 192 kHz on macOS 27.0.1, at 100% player volume. It also reports no automatic DAC rate switching, no hog mode, no FLAC/DSD, and a failed 32-bit test. The original third-party test report could not be independently rechecked for this update. The table attributes the 16/24-bit result to those tests and makes no claim about other configurations.

[Apple's lossless-audio support page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118295) documents ALAC playback. [Play high sample rate audio on your Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108326) says to choose the output rate manually in Audio MIDI Setup and "match the sample rate ... with the sample rate of your source material". Neither is the source of the macOS 27 bit-exact measurement. The [macOS 26](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/macos-release-notes/macos-26-release-notes) and macOS 27 release notes add no automatic rate switching.

Apple's [Spatial Audio guide for Music on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/music/play-spatial-audio-mus877f146ac/mac) covers Dolby Atmos tracks for Apple Music subscribers, with head tracking on AirPods. No Apple page documents surround or spatial playback of your own multichannel files, so local surround is "Not documented" and Spatial Audio is "Catalog Atmos only". The Music guide's [equalizer page](https://support.apple.com/guide/music/museb684a3de/mac) describes the only sound processing: the equalizer and Sound Enhancer. There is no convolution, Audio Unit effect hosting or file-analysis feature anywhere in the guide; Music's plug-ins are [visual effects](https://support.apple.com/guide/music/muse3aa39574/mac).

## Audirvana Studio and Origin

[Origin's product page](https://audirvana.com/audirvana-origin/) documents local FLAC and DSD, a one-time purchase without streaming services, and optional convolution. The supplied review covers native-rate/exclusive output and multichannel playback. [Audirvana's product page](https://audirvana.com/) distinguishes Studio's streaming integration from Origin's local library. [Audirvana's history](https://audirvana.com/about/) records AudioScan as a feature; hi-res analysis is not exclusive to Vespertine.

Audirvana's help center (articles marked as applying to Studio and Origin) documents [plugin hosting](https://help.audirvana.com/en/support/solutions/articles/202000051049-how-to-use-plugins-for-eq-or-acoustic-correction-in-audirv%C4%81na-) ("AudioUnits on macOS"), [supported formats](https://help.audirvana.com/en/support/solutions/articles/202000050817-which-audio-file-formats-are-compatible-with-audirv%C4%81na-) including DSF, DFF and ISO, [bit-perfect output by default](https://help.audirvana.com/en/support/solutions/articles/202000051094-is-audirv%C4%81na-bit-perfect-), automatic [exclusive access](https://help.audirvana.com/en/support/solutions/articles/202000072992-how-to-unlock-the-audio-ouput-), [AudioScan](https://help.audirvana.com/en/support/solutions/articles/202000051116-what-does-the-audioscan-results-mean-) and [multichannel playback to an AVR](https://help.audirvana.com/en/support/solutions/articles/202000059411-how-can-i-connect-my-device-to-audirv%C4%81na-). The [update history](https://help.audirvana.com/en/support/solutions/articles/202000096487-audirv%C4%81na-update-history) adds convolution in 2026, a paid option on Origin. Nothing in the help center or product pages mentions Apple Spatial Audio, head tracking or Dolby Atmos; Studio's Crossfeed is a stereo-only virtual-speaker effect ([Studio page](https://audirvana.com/audirvana-studio/)). Spatial Audio is therefore "Not documented". The help center doesn't state rate switching in so many words; the table's `Yes` rests on its bit-perfect article, which says Audirvana "sends the audio file unaltered to the audio device", something that can't happen without running the device at the file's rate.

## Roon

[Roon's audio setup guide](https://help.roonlabs.com/portal/en/kb/articles/audio-setup-basics) documents exclusive mode, DSD strategies and output controls. [Multichannel](https://help.roonlabs.com/portal/en/kb/articles/multichannel) documents local files with up to eight channels. [Convolution](https://help.roonlabs.com/portal/en/kb/articles/dsp-engine-convolution) documents filters. FLAC, rate switching and streaming-service integration are included in the supplied comparison review. [Roon's supported-formats FAQ](https://help.roonlabs.com/portal/en/kb/articles/faq-what-audio-file-formats-does-roon-support) lists DSD only "in the DSF and DFF file formats", so SACD ISO is shown as not supported. Its [sound-quality page](https://roon.app/en/sound-quality) states unaltered reproduction (a vendor claim). [Audio Analysis](https://help.roonlabs.com/portal/en/kb/articles/audio-analysis) measures loudness and peaks for volume leveling and "gathers information about the dynamic range of each track"; [Dynamic Range](https://help.roonlabs.com/portal/en/kb/articles/dynamic-range) defines it as R128 loudness range. Neither mentions detecting upsampled files, so the cell says what it measures. [MUSE](https://help.roonlabs.com/portal/en/kb/articles/muse) lists only built-in DSP, and Roon's co-founders have said on [Roon's forum](https://community.roonlabs.com/t/vst-plugins-in-roon-dsp/71531) that plugin hosting [is not planned](https://community.roonlabs.com/t/does-roon-support-will-support-vst-plugins/4653), so plugins are `No`. No Roon page mentions Dolby Atmos or Apple Spatial Audio, so that cell is "Not documented". Roon's [compatibility page](https://roon.app/en/compatibility/music) names TIDAL, Qobuz, KKBOX and nugs.net as streaming services. As with Audirvana, rate switching is implied by the [bit-perfect statement](https://roon.app/en/sound-quality) rather than stated directly.

## VeraVox

[VeraVox's FAQ](https://www.veravox.audio/faq.html) documents FLAC, DSD, SACD ISO, exclusive output and hi-res analysis. Its [technical documentation](https://www.veravox.audio/technical.html) and [exclusive-mode guide](https://www.veravox.audio/exclusive-mode-macos.html) describe the in-app bit-perfect loopback test. These are documented capabilities, not measurements made for this update. VeraVox is proprietary according to the supplied review and its [license](https://www.veravox.audio/eula.html). Its FAQ says it "does not perform room correction or convolution itself, by design", and its [home page](https://www.veravox.audio/) lists "No DSP" in the player path and "No streaming services. Local files and UPnP only". It can route to an external convolver through a virtual device. None of its pages mentions Apple Spatial Audio, AirPods or head tracking; on stereo devices multichannel files play the front left and right channels only, per the FAQ. Spatial Audio is therefore "Not documented".

## Colibri

[Colibri's product page](https://colibri-lossless.com/) lists FLAC and DSD. Its [FAQ](https://colibri-lossless.com/faq/) confirms automatic rate switching and says SACD ISO is unsupported. Its home page states exclusive/hog mode and "bit-perfect gapless playback" (a vendor claim). The FAQ says Qobuz and Spotify are not integrated; network radio is supported. Colibri is a paid [Mac App Store app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/colibri/id1178295426); neither its site nor its listing publishes source code or an open-source license, so it is shown as closed. Its site, FAQ, App Store description and version history (1.8.5 to 2.2.0) don't mention multichannel or surround files, Spatial Audio, file analysis, convolution or Audio Unit plugins, so those cells are "Not documented". It has a 10-band equalizer and BS2B crossfeed.

## Cog

[Cog's site](https://cog.losno.co/) describes a free, open-source player and lists FLAC, DSD, DTS, TrueHD and head tracking. Its October 4, 2026 release notes document surround spatialization with Apple's renderer. The table therefore includes local surround and Apple Spatial Audio. Vespertine does not claim to be the only player offering them. Its [README](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/README.md) (GPL) describes an exclusive mode that "runs [the output device] at each track's sample rate", and the app reports each track as bit perfect, modified or unknown. These were checked against the source at commit fb2ed48 (October 7, 2026). The license is GPL version 2 (`COPYING`). In the same source:

- No decoder registers an `.iso` extension; DSD comes only from DSF, DFF and WSD files (`Plugins/FFMPEG/FFMPEGDecoder.m`). SACD ISO is `No`.
- The only spectrum display is a live visualizer fed by the playing audio (`Audio/Engine/DSP/VisualizationTap.swift`); there is no dynamic-range or fake-hi-res analysis. File analysis is `No`.
- Spatialization uses Apple's `AUSpatialMixer`, and the equalizer is Cog's own biquad code (`Audio/Engine/DSP/EqualizerStage.swift`). Audio Units are loaded only as MIDI instruments, not effects. Convolution and plugins are `No`.
- The README lists "Streams over HTTP and HLS", with Shoutcast/Icecast metadata (`Plugins/HTTPSource/HTTPSource.m`); there is no streaming-service integration.

## foobar2000 for Mac

The [Mac download page](https://www.foobar2000.org/mac) and [Mac change log](https://www.foobar2000.org/changelog-mac) identify the Mac edition separately. The Mac change log documents FLAC support, "Exclusive audio output support" and "Apple Audio Unit DSP support" (both 2.5, April 2023). The [license](https://www.foobar2000.org/license) is proprietary ("All rights reserved"). Windows component support is not treated as Mac support. The Mac [formats page](https://www.foobar2000.org/formats) and change log don't mention DSD, DSF, DFF or SACD, and the [Mac component list](https://www.foobar2000.org/components/system/mac) has no DSD or SACD component. Because the vendor is silent, these cells were settled by a hands-on test on October 14, 2026: foobar2000 2.26 (build 75) on macOS 27.0.1, freshly installed with no components. A stereo DSD64 DSF, a DSDIFF (DFF) and a synthesized stereo SACD ISO each failed with "Unable to open item for playback (Unsupported file format)", while a FLAC control file played. Its built-in FFmpeg includes only the AC-3, DTS, TAK, WMA and APE readers. After installing FFmpeg separately (Homebrew) and adding `*.DSF;*.DFF` to Preferences > FFmpeg Decoder Wrapper, both files played as 352.8 kHz 32-bit float PCM, not native DSD or DoP. SACD ISO still failed, because FFmpeg can't read SACD images. The table therefore shows DSD and SACD ISO as `No`, with that footnote (users also report a third-party DSD component outside the official repository; it isn't counted). The change log doesn't say whether exclusive output switches the device rate, makes no bit-perfect claim, and doesn't mention multichannel output or Spatial Audio: all "Not documented". Version 2.25 added live visualisations ("VU Meter, Peak Meter, Spectrum, Oscilloscope"); no dynamic-range meter is available for Mac, so file analysis is "No (live spectrum only)". No convolver is documented; Audio Unit DSP is. Version 2.6 added internet radio search and 2.2.10 HLS radio; no streaming service is integrated.
